01 / The problem

A bill arrived. It does not explain itself.

Under the MLIT guideline, normal wear and aging (経年変化・通常損耗) is the landlord's cost. You pay for damage beyond normal use. Depreciation, consumables and cleaning clauses decide the rest — and your landlord must show concrete grounds for the deduction.

01

Wear is not damage

Faded wallpaper, worn flooring and everyday marks are normally the landlord's cost. Damage beyond normal use is the tenant's. The line between them is exactly what the statement rarely explains.

MLIT guideline ↗
02

The 6-year math

Wallpaper, cushion floor and carpet have a 6-year useful life under the guideline. The tenant's share falls linearly over the years lived, down to ¥1 at 6 years. Tatami facing, fusuma and shoji paper are consumables; partial flooring repair is not depreciated.

MLIT guideline ↗
03

Cleaning has a condition

A special cleaning clause (特約) can be valid when it was clearly explained and agreed. That is why a cleaning line cannot simply be deleted — the checker flags it so you can check the lease.

MLIT guideline ↗

You can ask for the itemized breakdown and the concrete grounds behind each deduction. Two recent r/japanlife threads describe the same moment: “Can I dispute the Moving out fee?” and “Excessive moving out fees from apartment.”
