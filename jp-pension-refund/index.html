Leaving Japan with pension money still on the table?

If you are finishing your JET contract and leaving for good with 6–60 months of Employees' Pension Insurance, there are two payments to understand: the lump-sum withdrawal and the 20.42% tax withheld from it. A plain-English, self-help kit is planned to help you claim both — without paying an agency.

The short version

  • 20.42% income tax withheld from the lump-sum withdrawal
  • 2 years to apply after leaving Japan

Figures from the Japan Pension Service and Kubota SR office. Estimates only; the Japan Pension Service decides the amount.

The problem

Two payments. Two clocks. One departure.

The refund is not one payment. It is a lump sum with tax already taken out, followed by a separate tax claim that depends on someone still living in Japan. Miss a step, and the money is easy to leave behind.

20.42% is withheld at source

JPS pays about 79.58% of the lump sum first. The rest is income tax withheld, recovered only after a tax representative in Japan files a return. The whole process takes about six months.

Source: Kubota SR office

The 2-year window is hard

Apply within two years after leaving Japan, once you are no longer covered. If your residence status is not cancelled, waiting for it to expire can push you past the deadline.

Sources: Japan Pension Service, Saitama leaving guide

The chain has many links

Pension number, tax representative form, moving-out notice, claim form, residence-card surrender, and the original decision notice sent back to Japan. Each one has its own timing.

Source: Saitama leaving guide

    The paid kit

    A start-to-finish workbook, designed around the official steps.

    The free demo shows the math. The paid kit will organise the whole process — what to do before you leave, what to send after, and how to follow the tax refund — in one place.

    • Route map: the official sequence from pre-departure preparation to the tax refund, with the Japanese form names and direct links.
    • Eligibility and scenario guide: the six conditions, plus branches for leaving soon, already left, lost documents, residence status not cancelled, and a claim near the deadline.
    • Document pack: pension number, claim form, tax representative form, 転出届, residence-card surrender, the original 脱退一時金支給決定通知書, and proof of mailing.
    • Tax representative worksheet: who can act, what to send, when to send it, and what they file.
    • Dated timeline planner: your 2-year deadline, the ~4-month first payment, the ~2-month tax refund, and the ~6-month total.
    • Worked examples: every payment-rate band, so you can sanity-check your own estimate.
    • Message templates and follow-up tracker: English messages to JPS and your tax representative, plus a simple log of what was sent and when.
    • Official source library and change watch: JPS, Saitama, MHLW and Kubota links, with the pending cap change clearly marked as pending.
    • FAQ: the confusions that show up in public posts — the tax-office step, a lost notice, and whether a tax representative is required.

    The free calculator stays free. The paid kit is a self-help workbook, not a filing service. It does not file claims, act as a tax representative, receive refunds, or give individual legal or tax advice.
