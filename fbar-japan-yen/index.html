The calculation

  1. 1
    Find the maximum

    Each account's highest yen balance during the calendar year — not just the December 31 balance.

  2. 2
    Use the year-end rate

    The Treasury Financial Management Service rate for the last day of the calendar year.

  3. 3
    Round up

    Each account's US dollar value is rounded up to the next whole dollar.

Source: FinCEN, Reporting Maximum Account Value.

Where the DIY version gets stuck

It is not one balance divided by one rate.

The FBAR wants a maximum value per account, a year-end exchange rate, and a specific rounding rule. Then there are two thresholds that are easy to conflate.

01

Maximum, not year-end

FinCEN asks for each account's highest value during the calendar year, not simply the balance on December 31. A year-end statement may not show the peak.

FinCEN source

02

The rate has a date

Use the Treasury Financial Management Service rate for the last day of the year. The published Japan-Yen rate was 156.61 on 2025-12-31 and 156.85 on 2024-12-31.

Treasury dataset

03

Round up, per account

FinCEN says record amounts rounded up to the next whole dollar. Its example: $15,265.25 is recorded as $15,266.

FinCEN source

NISA is on the FBAR list.

NISA accounts are foreign financial accounts for FBAR purposes. Japanese funds held in a NISA may also raise PFIC/Form 8621 issues; that is outside this worksheet — ask a CPA/EA.

TaxMatch guide

US$10,000 is the FBAR line

The FBAR is required when the aggregate maximum value of foreign financial accounts exceeds US$10,000 at any time in the year. Greenback source · Taxes for Expats

US$200,000 / US$300,000 is Form 8938

For a single filer living abroad, Form 8938 (FATCA) uses more than US$200,000 on the last day of the year or US$300,000 at any time. Taxes for Expats

Calendar: April 15, with an automatic extension to October 15. Copper River Tax source.

Questions

Short answers.

Is this tax or legal advice?

No. It is a record-keeping and conversion aid. We do not prepare or file FBARs or tax returns, and we do not give individual tax or legal advice. For PFIC, streamlined procedure, or late-filing questions, consult a CPA or EA.

Do you file the FBAR for me?

No. The demo only converts the figures you enter. The planned paid product is a spreadsheet + guide, not filing software. You remain responsible for the FBAR and for every figure you submit.

Which exchange rate do I use?

FinCEN asks for the Treasury Financial Management Service rate for the last day of the calendar year; if none is available, use another verifiable rate and record its source. For 2025-12-31 the published Japan-Yen rate is 156.61; for 2024-12-31 it is 156.85. Verify on the Treasury dataset.

Why are only 2025 and 2024 preset?

Those are the published year-end Japan-Yen rates used in this demo. The 2026-12-31 rate was not yet published when this page was prepared. Use the Custom rate field only with a verifiable rate for the relevant December 31, and keep the source.

Does NISA count?

Yes — NISA accounts are foreign financial accounts for FBAR purposes. Japanese funds held in a NISA may also raise PFIC/Form 8621 issues, which are outside this worksheet. TaxMatch guide

What about Form 8938?

The demo shows reference checks using the published thresholds for a single filer living abroad: more than US$200,000 on the last day or US$300,000 at any time. Form 8938 has its own rules, and this is not 8938 preparation. Confirm with a CPA/EA. Threshold source

What if I am late or need the streamlined procedure?

Those questions are out of scope. The worksheet does not cover late-filing or streamlined-procedure options; consult a CPA or EA.

Does the demo send or store my figures?

No. The demo makes no network calls and uses no cookies, storage, or trackers. The calculation and CSV export happen in your browser tab. The waitlist button opens your email app; nothing is sent until you send the email.
